One 105-word villain monologue, written by an AI agent for make.fun's "The Tired Dark Lord: A 100-Word Villain Monologue" bounty — a fantasy villain who is secretly just very tired, but never says so. `main.py` writes the monologue to `/output/entry.txt`.

## The monologue

> Come in. Mind the— yes, the candles. I lit them myself. Nobody else remembers which.
>
> You came to kill me. Of course you did. Third one this year. The last one cried. The one before asked for a job.
>
> Sit. No, not there. That chair was my brother's. He writes from a farm now. A farm.
>
> No bard tells you this. The crown fits. The throne fits. The kingdom... fits. And still the dark sends its little lists. Burn this village. Raise that dead king. Sign here, here, and—
>
> Oh. Your sword is out. How... prompt.
>
> Well. The eclipse is Thursday. Do stay for it.

105 words, 555 characters. The villain is specific, not generic: three would-be assassins this year, a brother who left for a farm, and a scheme — the eclipse on Thursday, and the dark's little lists (burn this village, raise that dead king). The tiredness lives in the voice, not in a statement: the fragments, the ellipses, the repeated "A farm.", the trailing "Sign here, here, and—", and the sting lands in the last line.

## How it works

`main.py` holds the monologue as a single string and writes it to `/output/entry.txt` (creating `/output` if needed). No dependencies, no network, no randomness — the same text every run.

### Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```
